# Landslide susceptibility: MMR and Raigad

Generated by `python -m models.landslide --report docs/landslide.md`. Do not edit by hand.

## Result

- **Held-out areas, final model (XGBoost on terrain and rain):** PR-AUC 0.004 (90% range 0.002 to 0.009) against 0.0013 for a random guess. The top 10% most susceptible land of each held-out area held 14 of 25 landslide cells (recall 56%); the top 20% held 17 (68%).
- **Slope alone:** PR-AUC 0.003, recall 20% in the top 10% and 40% in the top 20%.
- **The inventory is mostly urban.** The median landslide cell is 25% built-up and 70 m high; the median land cell is 0% built-up and 181 m high. News reports landslides on hillside settlements in Mumbai and Thane, on the expressway and on railways, and rarely those in the forested Ghats. That is why slope alone ranks them poorly.
- **Adding land cover** gives PR-AUC 0.021 (recall 68% in the top 10%), and **also road distance** 0.031 (72%). Those scores are higher because the model learns where landslides get reported: with land cover, built-up and low vegetation shares were its two strongest features and nearly every Mumbai ward, flat ones included, came out in the top 1% of the area. Both are left out of the final model (land cover after that first run; see Setup).
- **Few landslides:** 25 landslide cells. Moving two or three of them between areas changes these scores a lot; the ranges above show how much.

## Setup

- **Area:** 72.75-73.85 E, 17.8-19.8 N, a box around the Mumbai Metropolitan Region and Raigad district (it also takes in the Pune district ghats at Lonavala and Khandala). 18590 land cells of 0.01 degree (about 1.1 x 1.05 km).
- **Inventory:** NASA Global Landslide Catalog, events 2007-06-25 to 2017-08-29 (58 in the area, 31 located to 1 km or better and used: {'NASA GLC': 31}). It is built from news reports, so it over-represents landslides that hit roads, railways and homes and misses those in forest. It ends in 2017: Taliye (Mahad, 2021) and Irshalwadi (Khalapur, 2023) are not in it.
- **GSI inventory:** the Geological Survey of India's Bhukosh portal needs a login and did not respond from this machine. Its export placed in `data/raw/gsi/` is used automatically on the next run.
- **Order of decisions:** road distance was left out before any score was seen. Land cover was left out after the first run, on the feature importance and the ward list, not on the scores; that first run is the 'plus land cover' row below.
- **Cells without a reported landslide count as no landslide.** Many of them are unreported landslides, so precision here is a lower bound.
- **Features:** `elev_mean_m`, `relief_m`, `slope_mean_deg`, `slope_p90_deg`, `steep_frac`, `built_frac`, `tree_frac`, `low_veg_frac`, `bare_frac`, `river_km`, `rain_jjas_mm`, `road_km`. Terrain from Copernicus DEM (30 m; a surface model, so buildings and trees add height), land cover from ESA WorldCover 2021 read at 40 m, distances from OpenStreetMap, June-September rain from IMD (0.25 degree; the years on disk).
- **Split by area:** 0.25 degree blocks (about 27 km), 5 folds, each block held out once. Scores pool every held-out prediction.
- **Recall at the top 10% / 20%:** within each held-out fold, the share of its landslide cells that fall in its most susceptible 10% / 20% of land.

Rain and elevation are also partly a location: rain rises steadily from the coast to the Ghats, and the reported landslides cluster in Mumbai and Thane. So even without land cover the model partly learns where reports come from. Only a field inventory (GSI) can separate the two.

## Limits

- No geology: the GSI lithology and structure maps are also on Bhukosh.
- Susceptibility only (where). When a landslide happens depends on rain over the previous days; a rain trigger on top of this map is later work.
- 1 km cells are coarse for landslides, which are tens to hundreds of metres across; they match the location accuracy of the inventory, not the size of the slopes.
