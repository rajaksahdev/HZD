## How a replay works

- **Forecast replays (2024, 2025):** issued at 00:00 IST, 0 to 3 days before the flood day. Each hour's rain comes from an Open-Meteo archived forecast made at least that far ahead (Previous Runs archive: the forecast made 1 to 5 days before each hour), bias-corrected with the Stage 4 quantile map. IMD rain for a day counts as known from 12:00 IST that day (assumed publication time).
- **Observed replays (2005, 2021):** no forecast made a day or more ahead is archived for these years (the archive starts in 2024; for 2021 it holds GFS short-range forecasts only). They use observed IMD rain as a perfect forecast, so they measure no lead time.
- **Flagged** means the Stage 5 model gives 0.5 or more (orange or red on the map). The heavy-rain rule flags 64.5 mm or more in either 24 h window.
- **Lead time** for a reported ward is the largest number of days ahead at which it was flagged in every replay from that lead down to the day itself. Days are counted from 00:00 IST on the reported date; the flooding itself came some hours later.

## Limits

- Five flood days, three of them with archived forecasts. These are examples, not a skill estimate.
- The quantile map was learned on short-range forecasts; it is applied unchanged to forecasts made days ahead.
- The exact start time of each archived run is not given, only that it was made 24K to 24K+24 hours ahead, so a replay may use a run a few hours older than a live system would have had.
- The model inherits the Stage 5 problem: it flags nearly any rainy day, so long lead times come with many warning days. Compare the model and rule columns, not lead time alone.
